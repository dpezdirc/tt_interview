D: Can you walk us through the process of composing and implementing a piece of music into a game made for the Famicom?


T: First, I visualize the music I’m about to create all the way through to its final form.

Then, I establish an arrangement strategy tailored to the characteristics and limitations of the hardware that I'm working with. For example, “How can I express this using the Famicom’s 4-voice PSG or the Super Famicom’s 8-voice system?” During this stage, I also sketch out the sheet music.

That initial phase of establishing that musical vision is crucial. Only once that's solidified can you move on the programming phase, which involves inputting the performance data using a computer or similar tool. Tempo, phrasing, and articulation - that data all needs to be carefully specified for every note.

In my case, I did everything from start to finish, including the implementation itself.
